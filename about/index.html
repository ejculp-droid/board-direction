About Board Direction

Good People. Important Work. Better Direction.

We believe capable people can come together for the right reasons and still benefit from a better way of working. Board Direction helps make that work more purposeful, productive, and impactful.

A board's value should be reflected in the clarity of its direction, the quality of its decisions, and the progress made between meetings, not just the hours its members spend together.

Years of experience has taught us that good intentions, fancy titles and annual social events alone do not translate into board success.

Board Members join boards for various reasons, have varying skill sets and varying degrees of committable time and energy. Which is okay and expected.

Our purpose is simple: help boards spend less time managing their people and processes, and instead, more time advancing their purposes.

Board Direction begins by listening.

The goal is to understand what your board is trying to accomplish, recognize what is already working, and identify where greater clarity, alignment, or better processes could make a difference.

The approach is collaborative, respectful, and practical. It isn't about introducing complexity or replacing the judgment of those already serving.

It's about helping that judgment translate into better use of time, clearer responsibilities, and progress that continues between meetings.

Seeking an outside perspective isn't an admission that something is wrong. It's a commitment to making something important work better.

You don't need to spend more time on your board. You need to make more of the time you already give.

Board Direction collaborates with boards ready to align their people and priorities, clarify responsibilities and expectations, and improve the structures and processes that turn decisions into action.

Founded by Elliott J. Culp, Board Direction is grounded in firsthand experience serving on boards, working alongside them, helping lead and develop them, and shaping the structures and practices that support effective governance.

That experience is complemented by a broad foundation in executive advisory, organizational development, leadership, strategic planning, and organizational decision-making.

Together, these perspectives provide an understanding of governance that extends beyond the meeting room. We understand the relationships, responsibilities, competing priorities, and organizational realities that influence how boards function and how their decisions take effect.
